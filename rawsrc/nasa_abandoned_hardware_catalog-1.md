# NASA's Abandoned / Left-Behind Hardware Across the Solar System

**Research date:** 19 September 2026

## Scope

This is a research-oriented catalog of **NASA hardware that was
intentionally left behind, became inactive on another world, crashed
into a planetary body, or is now an inactive spacecraft continuing
through deep space**.

It is **not possible to prove a literally complete inventory of every
nut, bolt, wrapper, experiment component, or debris fragment**. NASA
itself notes that some historical item lists are not exhaustive. The
catalog below therefore focuses on **named missions and documented major
hardware**, with smaller documented equipment grouped under the mission
that left it.

For the NASA Space Apps Challenge concept in the supplied prompt, the
most useful categories are:

-   **Moon:** abandoned surface equipment, landers, rovers, experiments
    and impact debris.
-   **Mars:** inactive landers/rovers/helicopter plus discarded landing
    hardware.
-   **Other worlds / deep space:** spacecraft deliberately crashed into
    worlds, landed and became inactive, or continue silently on escape
    trajectories.

# 1. Moon

## 1.1 Apollo 11 --- Tranquility Base

**Date:** July 20, 1969\
**Status:** Hardware remains on the lunar surface.

Major items left behind:

-   **Apollo 11 Lunar Module Eagle descent stage** --- the largest major
    object left at Tranquility Base.
-   **Early Apollo Scientific Experiments Package (EASEP)**:
    -   Passive Seismic Experiment Package
    -   Laser Ranging Retro-Reflector
-   **Lunar surface television camera and support equipment**
-   **Solar Wind Composition experiment / collector**
-   **Apollo lunar hand tools**, including tools used for sample
    collection
-   **Portable Life Support System (PLSS) equipment**
-   **Lunar boots and other expendable equipment**
-   **American flag**
-   **Plaque attached to the Lunar Module**
-   **Gnomon**
-   **Various bags, packaging and jettisoned consumables**

NASA's Apollo 11 mission log explicitly identifies the descent stage, TV
camera, still cameras, sample-collection tools, PLSS equipment, lunar
boots, flag, solar-wind experiment hardware, laser reflector, seismic
detector and gnomon among the items left behind.

**Why it matters:** Tranquility Base is one of the most historically
significant human-made archaeological sites outside Earth.

Sources: -

https://nssdc.gsfc.nasa.gov/planetary/lunar/apollo_11_30th.html -
https://www.nasa.gov/history/55-years-ago-apollo-11s-one-small-step-one-giant-leap/

## 1.2 Apollo 12 --- Oceanus Procellarum

**Date:** November 19, 1969\
**Status:** Hardware remains on the lunar surface.

Major surviving/left hardware:

-   **Lunar Module Intrepid descent stage**
-   **Apollo 12 ALSEP** (Apollo Lunar Surface Experiments Package)
-   **PLSS backpacks**
-   **S-band antenna**
-   **American flag**
-   **Scientific instruments and cables**
-   **Jettison bags and expendable equipment**
-   **Remaining Apollo surface tools/equipment**
-   **Surveyor 3 spacecraft**, which was already on the Moon and was
    inspected by Apollo 12

ALSEP included instruments such as the passive seismometer, suprathermal
ion detector, cold cathode ion gauge, charged-particle experiment, lunar
surface magnetometer and solar-wind spectrometer.

Apollo 12 is especially interesting because astronauts landed about 600
feet from Surveyor 3 and removed several Surveyor components for return
to Earth. The rest of Surveyor 3 remains on the Moon.

## 1.7 Apollo ALSEP network

The **Apollo Lunar Surface Experiments Package (ALSEP)** is worth
treating as its own category because it represents a collection of
long-lived scientific hardware rather than one spacecraft.

Installed at:

-   Apollo 12
-   Apollo 14
-   Apollo 15
-   Apollo 16
-   Apollo 17

The packages contained combinations of:

-   seismometers
-   heat-flow experiments
-   magnetometers
-   solar-wind instruments
-   charged-particle instruments
-   ion detectors
-   dust/thermal experiments
-   central stations
-   cables
-   radioisotope thermoelectric generators

The ALSEP instruments operated from their deployments in 1969--1972
until NASA shut the network down on **September 30, 1977**.

Sources: - https://ntrs.nasa.gov/citations/20180008613 -
https://ntrs.nasa.gov/citations/20205005872 -
https://science.nasa.gov/planetary-science/programs/radioisotope-power-systems/missions/

# 3. NASA Ranger impact spacecraft

Ranger spacecraft were deliberately designed to transmit close-up images
and then **impact the Moon**.

Known NASA Ranger spacecraft that ended by reaching the lunar surface
include:

-   **Ranger 4** --- impacted the Moon in 1962 but failed to return
    useful science.
-   **Ranger 6** --- impacted the Moon in 1964; camera system failed.
-   **Ranger 7** --- impacted in 1964 after returning thousands of
    images.
-   **Ranger 8** --- impacted in 1965 after returning more than 7,000
    images.
-   **Ranger 9** --- impacted in 1965 after returning thousands of
    images.

Ranger 7, 8 and 9 were the successful final impact missions of the
series.

Sources: -
https://science.nasa.gov/mission/lunar-rangers-and-surveyors/ -
https://nssdc.gsfc.nasa.gov/planetary/lunar/ranger.html -
https://science.nasa.gov/resource/ranger-9-approaches-the-moon/

# 4. Later NASA Moon impact hardware

## Lunar Prospector

**Impact:** July 31, 1999

NASA deliberately crashed Lunar Prospector near the Moon's south pole at
the end of its mission. The controlled impact was intended to look for
evidence of water ice in the resulting plume.

**Left behind:** spacecraft/debris from the Lunar Prospector impact.

Source: - https://science.nasa.gov/mission/lunar-prospector/ -
https://science.nasa.gov/solar-system/moon/creative-impact-experiment-to-mark-end-of-lunar-prospector/

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## LCROSS

**Impact:** October 9, 2009

Two major pieces of NASA/launch hardware ended up at the Cabeus crater:

1.  **Centaur upper stage** --- intentionally crashed first.
2.  **LCROSS Shepherding Spacecraft** --- followed through the plume and
    then impacted the Moon.

The impact created a plume that was studied for water and other
materials.

Source: - https://science.nasa.gov/mission/lcross/ -
https://svs.gsfc.nasa.gov/3654/

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## GRAIL --- Ebb and Flow

**Impact:** December 17, 2012

The twin NASA spacecraft **Ebb** and **Flow** were deliberately
deorbited into the Moon after completing their gravity-mapping mission.

Both spacecraft struck the lunar surface near an unnamed mountain close
to the craters Mouchez and Philolaus.

Source: - https://science.nasa.gov/mission/grail/ -
https://science.nasa.gov/resource/grail-impacts-the-moon/

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## LADEE

**Impact:** April 18, 2014

NASA's Lunar Atmosphere and Dust Environment Explorer was deliberately
sent into the lunar surface after completing its science mission.

**Left behind:** the LADEE spacecraft and impact debris.

Source: - https://science.nasa.gov/mission/ladee/ -
https://www.nasa.gov/news-release/nasa-completes-ladee-mission-with-planned-impact-on-moons-surface/

## 5.8 Curiosity --- discarded landing hardware

**Landing:** August 6, 2012\
**Rover status:** Active as of the research date.

Curiosity itself is **not abandoned**, but several pieces of its landing
system are permanently left on Mars:

-   heat shield
-   backshell
-   parachute
-   descent stage
-   entry ballast masses / associated landing hardware

NASA's orbital imagery identified the descent stage, rover, backshell,
parachute and heat shield at the landing site.

## 5.9 Perseverance --- discarded landing hardware

**Landing:** February 18, 2021\

## 5.10 Ingenuity

**First flight:** April 19, 2021\
**Final flight:** January 18, 2024\
**Status:** Permanently grounded on Mars.

Ingenuity remains upright on the Martian surface. NASA ended the flight
mission after rotor damage during its 72nd flight made further flight
impossible.

Major remaining hardware:

-   Ingenuity helicopter
-   rotor system
-   solar panel
-   avionics
-   batteries
-   landing legs
-   communications hardware

This is a particularly strong storytelling candidate because it is **not
a crash site**: the first aircraft to achieve powered, controlled flight
on another planet is sitting where it completed its final flight.

Sources: -
https://www.nasa.gov/news-release/after-three-years-on-mars-nasas-ingenuity-helicopter-mission-ends/ -
https://science.nasa.gov/blog/farewell-to-our-flying-friend-and-closing-in-on-the-crater-rim/

# 6. Other NASA hardware left on other worlds

The challenge description says "Moon and Mars" but also mentions
hardware "in deep space" and "across the solar system." The following
missions broaden the story beyond those two worlds.

## 6.1 NEAR Shoemaker --- Asteroid Eros

**Landing:** February 12, 2001\
**Status:** Spacecraft remains on asteroid 433 Eros.

NEAR was not designed as a lander. NASA nevertheless successfully
brought it down onto Eros after completing its orbital mission. It
survived the landing and returned data for roughly two weeks before
contact was lost.

**Left behind:** NEAR Shoemaker spacecraft on Eros.

Sources: - https://science.nasa.gov/mission/near-shoemaker/ -
https://www.jpl.nasa.gov/news/jpl-navigators-guide-near-to-historic-landing-on-asteroid-eros/

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## 6.2 MESSENGER --- Mercury

**Impact:** April 30, 2015\
**Status:** Spacecraft impacted Mercury.

MESSENGER orbited Mercury for more than four years and ended its mission
by impacting the planet after running out of propellant.

**Left behind:** spacecraft debris and an impact crater.

Source: - https://science.nasa.gov/mission/messenger/

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## 6.3 Magellan --- Venus

**Impact:** October 1994\
**Status:** Spacecraft burned up in Venus' atmosphere.

Magellan mapped almost the entire Venusian surface using radar. NASA
then commanded it into the atmosphere, where it burned up.

**Left behind:** surviving atmospheric/impact debris, if any; most of
the spacecraft was destroyed in the atmosphere.

Source: - https://science.nasa.gov/mission/magellan/

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## 6.4 Galileo --- Jupiter

**Impact:** September 21, 2003\
**Status:** Destroyed in Jupiter's atmosphere.

NASA intentionally sent Galileo into Jupiter to prevent an uncontrolled
future impact with Europa.

**Left behind:** spacecraft material deposited/destroyed in Jupiter's
atmosphere.

Source: - https://www.jpl.nasa.gov/news/galileo-end-of-mission-status/

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## 6.5 Cassini --- Saturn

**Impact:** September 15, 2017\
**Status:** Destroyed in Saturn's atmosphere.

NASA intentionally plunged Cassini into Saturn at the end of its mission
to avoid contaminating potentially habitable moons such as Enceladus.

**Left behind:** spacecraft material entering Saturn's atmosphere.

Source: -
https://www.nasa.gov/news-release/nasa-cassini-spacecraft-ends-its-historic-exploration-of-saturn/

# 8. Important distinction: inactive vs abandoned

For a storytelling project, these categories should not be mixed.

### A. Permanently sitting on a planetary surface

Examples:

-   Apollo LM descent stages
-   Apollo ALSEP
-   Apollo lunar rovers
-   Surveyor landers
-   Viking landers
-   Pathfinder/Sojourner
-   Spirit
-   Opportunity
-   Phoenix
-   InSight
-   Ingenuity
-   NEAR Shoemaker

### B. Deliberately crashed into a world

Examples:

-   Ranger 4/6/7/8/9
-   Lunar Prospector
-   LCROSS + Centaur
-   GRAIL Ebb + Flow
-   LADEE
-   MESSENGER
-   Galileo
-   Cassini

### C. Landing-system debris left behind while the main spacecraft continues

Examples:

-   Curiosity heat shield, backshell, parachute and descent stage
-   Perseverance heat shield, backshell, parachute and descent stage
-   Phoenix parachute, backshell and heat shield

### D. Inactive spacecraft drifting through space

Examples:

-   Pioneer 10
-   Pioneer 11

### E. Still operational --- do NOT call these abandoned

As of the research date, examples include:

-   Curiosity
-   Perseverance
-   Mars Reconnaissance Orbiter
-   Mars Odyssey
-   Voyager 1
-   Voyager 2
-   New Horizons

# 10. NASA's broader source catalogs

For expanding this into a truly exhaustive database, start with NASA's
**NSSDCA Master Catalog** and NASA's lunar artifact/history resources.

NASA's NSSDCA Master Catalog contains mission information for NASA
planetary missions involving Mercury, Venus, the Moon, Mars, asteroids,
Jupiter, Saturn, Uranus, Neptune and Pluto.

Sources:

-   https://nssdc.gsfc.nasa.gov/planetary/projectdata.html
-   https://nssdc.gsfc.nasa.gov/planetary/chrono.html
-   https://www.nasa.gov/wp-content/uploads/static/history/items_carried.html

## Important limitation

NASA's own "Items Carried in Space" resource says its collections are
**not exhaustive** and are intended as a starting point for researchers.
Therefore, this document should be treated as a **curated major-hardware
catalog**, not a legal or archaeological inventory of every human-made
object on another world.
